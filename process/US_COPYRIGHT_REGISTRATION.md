# US Copyright Office registration — the working plan

*Researched 2026-09-12 against copyright.gov primary sources (statute,
Circulars, the Compendium, the 2026 fee rulemaking, and the March 2023
AI guidance). This is a checklist, not legal advice. The ledger item is
TODO-NIELS §5; this file is what that row points at.*

**The book: first published 2026-08-25, ISBN 978-87-977519-1-6, 206 pp
paperback, own imprint, printed print-on-demand, listed on amazon.com
(live 2026-08-27) and amazon.de.**

## Three corrections to what the ledger assumed

1. **The decision deadline is late September / early October — not
   mid-November.** The three-month date (~2026-11-25) is right, but what
   must happen by it is *receipt* of application **+ fee + deposit copy**
   (17 U.S.C. §410(d)). The deposit is almost certainly a **printed book
   mailed from Denmark**, and all mail to the Office is screened offsite
   first, adding unquantified delay. Work back four to six weeks.
2. **It is the Standard Application ($65), not the Single ($45).** The
   Single Application has no field in which AI-generated material can be
   disclaimed, and sixteen AI plates must be disclaimed. Filing Single is
   **refused, not corrected** — refile, new fee, **new and later effective
   date**. That is the single most expensive mistake available here.
3. **Fees rise on or about 2026-11-11** (first change since 2020):
   Standard $65 → $85. Not a reason to hurry by itself; it points the
   same way as everything else.

## Decision 0, and it sets everything else: was the book published in the US?

The test is not nationality — it is **place of first publication**. The
Office's own rule: a work published simultaneously in the US and abroad
"is considered published in the United States" (Circular 2), and the
eCO help says to enter **United States** as Nation of First Publication
in that case.

Our own record says the amazon.com listing went live **two days after**
the 25 August publication date, with the .de listing the same week. On
those facts this reads as a **US publication**, which means:

- **Nation of First Publication: United States** on the form;
- **best-edition rules apply → a physical copy must be mailed.** The PDF
  upload route is open only to works published *solely* outside the US.
- A separate obligation comes with it: **§407 mandatory deposit** to the
  Library of Congress, which applies to US-published works whether or not
  they are registered. **The registration deposit satisfies both at
  once.** (Unrelated to, and no substitute for, the Danish
  pligtaflevering — see `SEND_BOOKS_KB_DBC.md`.)

Niels makes this call; the Office will discuss it on +1 202-707-3000 if
the timing is genuinely borderline. Getting it wrong means the wrong
deposit route, correspondence, and a moved effective date.

## What registration buys, stated honestly

- **§412 statutory damages ($750–$150,000/work) and attorney's fees** for
  infringements beginning after the effective date — and retroactively to
  publication if registered inside the three months. There is **no
  foreign-author exemption in §412**: identical footing with a US author.
- **§410(c)** prima facie validity if registered within five years.
- It removes an argument rather than winning one on **§411(a) standing**:
  courts split on whether a simultaneous worldwide print-on-demand release
  makes this a "United States work" requiring registration before suit.
  $65 makes the question moot.
- **The AI-training-settlement point, which is the real reason to lean
  yes — and its limits.** In *Bartz v. Anthropic* the certified class
  required US registration within five years of publication **and** before
  the download or within three months of publication. Registration was
  written into the class definition, so unregistered books were simply
  out; ~500,000 titles survived from ~7 million copies. *Kadrey v. Meta*
  and *Hendrix v. Apple* copy the formula. **But:** registration is close
  to necessary and nowhere near sufficient, and *Bartz* itself is
  categorically irrelevant to this book — those downloads were 2021–22,
  before it existed, and claims closed March 2026. The argument is
  entirely prospective, about corpora scraped from 2027 on. What
  registering now buys is the "registered before the infringement
  commenced" prong, permanently.

## The AI disclosure — where the book's honesty rule and the legal rule agree

Governing text is still *Copyright Registration Guidance: Works
Containing Material Generated by Artificial Intelligence*, 88 FR 16190
(March 2023), unamended as of September 2026; the promised supplement has
not issued, and the Supreme Court's March 2026 cert denial in *Thaler*
entrenched it. The duty to **disclose** has no threshold; the duty to
**exclude** applies to anything more than de minimis. Sixteen plates are
not de minimis.

On the Limitation of Claim screen (Standard Application only):

| field | entry |
|---|---|
| Author Created | check **Text**; "Selection, coordination, and arrangement of text created by the author and artwork generated by artificial intelligence" |
| Material Excluded | check **Artwork**; in *Other*: "Illustrations generated by artificial intelligence" |
| Previous Registration | blank |
| New Material Included | check **Text**; mirror the Author Created wording |
| Note to Copyright Office | name the tool, say which plates are AI-generated and how much human editing each had |

Two mechanical traps: eCO **rejects** the application if Material
Excluded is filled and New Material Included is not, and the combined
free text is capped at **1,800 characters**.

**The TikZ diagrams are human-authored and must NOT be excluded.** Say so
explicitly in the Note to CO — the exclusion is the AI plates, not the
figures. Where a plate was substantively hand-edited, describe the edit;
the Office has accepted such edits as human authorship, and vagueness
forfeits that. On the disclosure already made to KDP ("many, minimal
editing"), the plates land on the excluded side.

What this leaves protected: **the text** — which is the asset — plus the
selection, coordination and arrangement. The illustrations would be
unprotected by the registration. The exclusion prints on the certificate
and in the public catalogue. Class TX.

The cost of not disclosing is documented: *Zarya of the Dawn* was
cancelled and reissued, and the public record permanently carries
"failure to exclude non-human authorship". It also hands a future
defendant a §411(b) argument. The book's standing rule is that
concessions stay on the page; this is the same move.

## Privacy — decide the address BEFORE typing it

**The claimant address appears in the online public catalogue**, the
Office will not remove it without a substitute physical address, and
removal from the catalogue does not remove it from offline records open
to public inspection (Circular 18). The Office's own advice is to use a
PO box or third-party address. Given the scrub history on this project,
treat this as effectively irreversible and settle it first.

## Sequence

1. Settle the public address (PO box or equivalent).
2. Create the eCO account at copyright.gov/registration — allow pop-ups;
   Pay.gov and the shipping slip both open in new windows.
3. **Order a spare author copy now** — it has its own lead time, and the
   deposit copy is given up permanently. (Stock is currently one at home
   plus the two the KB resend needs.)
4. File the **Standard Application**, class TX, Limitation of Claim as
   above. Budget 60–90 minutes; drafts save.
5. Pay **$65** via Pay.gov by card. **Have a second card ready** — whether
   Pay.gov reliably accepts a Danish-issued card is not documented
   anywhere official, so test it early, never on the deadline.
6. Print the **shipping slip** from the Case Summary screen and attach it
   to the book. Without it the deposit cannot be matched to the
   application: a per-half-hour location fee, and if it is lost, a new
   deposit and **a new effective date**.
7. Post **one copy + slip in a single package**, tracked, to the address
   printed on the slip (not a remembered address). **Target: mailed by
   mid-October.** A courier costs $40–70 more and buys proof of delivery
   on the date that matters.
8. Optionally also upload the PDF, stating in the Note to CO that it is
   archival and the hard copy follows separately.
9. Wait. Roughly 4.4 months to a certificate for mailed-deposit claims,
   6.2 if the examiner corresponds (27% do). The effective date is
   already fixed at receipt, so **do not buy $800 special handling** — it
   would not move it.

**Total: ~$90–110 (DKK 600–750), two to three hours at the keyboard, one
trip to the post office.** A small job with a hard date on it.

## Open, and flagged rather than guessed

- The **exact** fee change date — the Office says "fall 2026"; the
  statutory earliest is ~2026-11-11; Congress could still disapprove.
- Whether **Pay.gov accepts a Danish card**. No official source says
  either way.
- How long **offsite mail screening** adds. The Office says it delays
  delivery and does not say by how much — the reason for the buffer.
