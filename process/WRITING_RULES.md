# WRITING_RULES --- the reference law (adopted 2026-08-12)
Origin: Niels's finding that machine prose, written from total recall, under-supplies
the context refresh a human buffer needs. Adopted for all future prose; applied
retroactively by the context pass (P8, record/).

1. THE BOUNDARY RULE. A bare anaphor (it, that, this, they, the two...) may reach back
   only within its own paragraph, and only when no competing referent stands nearer
   than the true one. Across any boundary --- paragraph, figure, table, a
   likely page turn --- the referent is re-nouned.
2. NUMBERS ARE NEVER NAMES. "Two" and "one" as referents always carry their nouns:
   not "two pull it down" but "the heat books and the transparency press the stopping
   chance down."
3. REFRESH BY RESTATEMENT-WITH-ANGLE. The re-noun should compress or advance the
   argument, so the fast reader feels progress and the slow reader gets rescue. Never
   annotation-parentheses for reference repair; parentheses are reserved for the
   voice's own asides and prices. One licensed exception, author-flagged per instance:
   the reference footnote --- for a sentence whose rhythm the author refuses to touch,
   a footnote in the fixed micro-format "The two purchases: the hidden frame; lower
   layers are faster." may carry the refresh instead. The rule repairs in prose; the
   exception protects sentences, sparingly.
4. CHRISTEN ONCE, THEN NAME. Load-bearing ideas get a name at birth (the pincer, the
   valve, the passenger, the fork) and are called by it forever; a name is a refresh
   that costs one word.
5. THE CALIBRATION GUARD. Within a paragraph, trust the reader; across one, help them
   --- and inside the book's longest paragraphs, treat a reach of many sentences as a
   boundary too: the buffer decays without a line break's help.
   No "as you recall" tics; if a repair re-explains rather than restates, it insults.
6. ENUMERATIONS ANNOUNCE THEMSELVES. "The first: ..." may not arrive before the reader
   has been told how many are coming and of what.
7. PATCH HYGIENE. Any insertion (parenthesis, paragraph, figure) can displace a
   neighbouring anaphor from its referent; every future patch checks the first
   sentence AFTER its insertion point against rule 1 before shipping.
