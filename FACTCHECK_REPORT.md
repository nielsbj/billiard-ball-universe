## Discrepancies (prose untouched — your call)

**D1. Tegmark figure (Ch. 4 and Ch. 5).**
Book, Ch. 4: *"Tegmark did the distance arithmetic: your nearest exact copy is on the order of $10^{10^{118}}$ meters away."*
Book, Ch. 5: *"Tegmark did the arithmetic for our own layer: your nearest copy is roughly $10^{10^{118}}$ meters away…"*
Source (arXiv:astro-ph/0302131, §2, abstract): nearest copy of **you** ≈ **10^{10^{29}} m**; nearest identical **Hubble volume** ≈ **10^{10^{115}} m** (the 2014 book's packing argument gives 10^{10^{118}} m for the Hubble-volume figure).
→ The book attaches the Hubble-volume exponent to the personal-copy claim. Either switch to 10^{10^{29}} m for "your nearest copy," or rephrase to "an exact copy of our entire observable volume" and keep ~10^{10^{115–118}} m. Flagged in the Ch. 5 craft note.

**D2. Sailors/two-ships lore (Ch. 6).**
Boersma (1996) presents the analogy, but Pinto (2006) showed Boersma's 1836 nautical source describes ships attracting in a **calm** sea (not a swell) and found no documented observations of the effect in sailing or scientific literature. The wave-mediated physics is sound; the "sailors knew this" framing is contested. Flagged in the Ch. 6 craft note; `boersma1996` entry added with the caution.
